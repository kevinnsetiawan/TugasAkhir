Dear Editor-in-Chief,

I am pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "Automated Web Penetration Testing with Reinforcement Learning: A Comparative Study of Q-Learning, Deep Q-Network, and OWASP ZAP" for consideration for publication in the Journal of Information Security and Applications.

This paper presents a rigorous comparative study of reinforcement learning approaches for automated web application penetration testing. Our work makes several contributions that we believe are well-aligned with JISA's scope:

1. We formulate web penetration testing as an MDP with an 8-dimensional state space and a composite action space of 88 payload-endpoint combinations, enabling endpoint-aware policy learning that prior work has not addressed.

2. We provide the first direct three-way comparison of tabular Q-Learning, Deep Q-Network, and the industry-standard OWASP ZAP on identical web endpoints, with controlled experimental conditions and reproducible configurations.

3. We introduce a false-positive verification mechanism using benign probes that improves detection precision without sacrificing recall.

4. Our experimental results demonstrate that Q-Learning achieves a 91.5% detection rate (183 vulnerabilities in 200 requests, 1.09 requests per vulnerability) — a 727-fold improvement in efficiency over OWASP ZAP.

The findings address a critical gap in the literature: while prior studies evaluate RL agents either in simulated network environments or without direct comparison to standard scanners, our work establishes quantitative efficiency benchmarks on real web applications.

We confirm that this manuscript has not been published elsewhere and is not under consideration by another journal. All authors have approved the manuscript and agree with its submission to JISA.

We appreciate your consideration and look forward to your response.
